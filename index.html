ESCALATION

Wellness Tech · Low Utilization & Retention Crisis

+

A corporate HR team flagged their subscription for cancellation — employee participation had dropped to 8%, and they couldn’t justify the spend internally. I stepped in as the single point of contact, diagnosed usage patterns by country and class type, and coordinated a recovery plan with Operations, Product, and leadership. We rolled out country-specific class schedules, replaced underperforming instructors, and launched a targeted activation campaign — with weekly usage reports the HR team could use to demonstrate ROI.

  • Participation increased from 8% → 20% within 60 days
  • Account retained at full contract value through renewal
  • HR received measurable proof of ROI to defend the spend internally
  • Recovery framework adopted for similar at-risk accounts in the portfolio
PRODUCT

B2B SaaS · Workflow Gap Driving Churn Risk

+

A customer arranging international pet transportation was frustrated by the lack of visibility into documentation and travel readiness — they relied on manual emails to track what was complete. I mapped their full workflow, documented where the platform forced manual coordination, and brought it to Product and Operations with specific breakdown examples. The customer stayed and renewed, and the workflow insight was pitched to Product as a scalable feature.

  • Retained a $200K+ ARR account evaluating alternative transportation services
  • Interim readiness checklist deployed immediately for upcoming shipment
  • Customer renewed at full contract value with confidence restored

RENEWAL

Pet Logistics · One-Off Failure to Scalable Fix

The customer regained confidence and continued using transportation, while the updated process reduced similar friction for subsequent shipments.

QBR

PetTech Services · Strategic QBR Driving Renewal + Expansion

+

A cattery customer was up for renewal with healthy transportation usage but zero adoption of our nutrition consultation service. Instead of treating the QBR as a renewal formality, I reviewed their customer journey and discovered their team was fielding frequent post-placement feeding questions from kitten buyers — work our nutrition specialists could handle. I restructured the QBR around this untapped need, brought in the nutrition service lead, and proposed a 90-day pilot offering consultations to their recent customers. The pilot showed strong adoption, and the customer renewed their transportation contract while adding the nutrition service.

  • Renewal closed with cross-service expansion into nutrition consultations — $30K+ in new ARR
  • 90-day pilot converted into permanent contract addition
  • Account value increased, contributing to 110%+ NRR
  • QBR format adopted as a template for identifying expansion opportunities in other accounts

Operating Philosophy

How I Work

Proactive by Default

I don’t wait for renewal dates to have strategic conversations or for tickets to surface before checking on flagged accounts. I run weekly health score reviews across my 25–30 accounts and act on anything that needs attention before it becomes an escalation — this approach reduced quarterly churn risk by 15% prior to executive involvement.

Prepared for Every Conversation

Every customer meeting gets a 30-day lookback: what was discussed, what was promised, what changed. I bring specific talking points and usage data. When a QBR is on the calendar, I align with leadership on goals, metrics, and next steps before we walk in the room.

Own the Escalation

When something goes wrong, I own it end to end. I’ve built cross-functional escalation pathways that reduce time-to-resolution by 25%, keep all stakeholders aligned, and stay in the conversation until the issue is resolved — with a written summary to the customer on what happened and what changed.

Build and Share

Every process, template, and workflow I create is documented and available to the team. I authored standardized FAQ templates and support macros that reduced repetitive ticket volume by 20%. Good systems should not live in one person’s head — I build to scale, not to protect territory.
